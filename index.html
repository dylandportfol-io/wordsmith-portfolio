The frontier: what AI can and cannot do for legal

Deploying Wordsmith in regulated environments requires a clear understanding of progressive disclosure and machine limits.

● What Wordsmith delivers today

  • Auto routing and triage: Reaches across shared inboxes and Jira via Model Context Protocol (MCP) to parse unstructured intake and route tasks to the correct legal owner.
  • RAG-enabled policy synthesis: Utilises retrieval augmented generation (RAG) to anchor outputs directly to internal compliance matrices, ISO controls, and FCA guidance synced in the repository.
  • First-draft evidence compilation: Assembles complex enhanced due diligence (EDD) packs and audit registers in standard-compliant language using prescribed playbooks.

● Current guardrails

  • Unsupervised regulatory discretion: Wordsmith cannot replace senior legal liability. It surfaces defensible rationale via skills, but final sign-off remains with qualified counsel.
  • Autonomous state-changing actions: High-liability external dispatches (e.g., notifying the ICO under Article 33 or FCA STOR filings) are structurally blocked without a HITL review gate.
  • Subjective risk appetite calibration: AI cannot determine an enterprise's commercial tolerance for borderline sanctions nexus without codified boundaries in the playbook.
